Amazon / Flipkart / Meesho — how to buy safely
  • Buy only branded modules — Loom Solar, Waaree, Luminous, ZunSolar, Microtek, Exide. The listing must state 12 V nominal, 36 cells and give Voc (should be 22–26 V).
  • Meesho caution: most listings there are unbranded/B-grade with no serviceable warranty. Buy from Meesho only if the seller is a brand's own store and the price is not suspiciously low; returns on 12 kg glass panels are painful.
  • Unbox in front of the delivery person. Photograph the carton and panels; refuse any box with a cracked cell or bent frame. Micro-cracks show up as whitish lines under light.
  • Keep the GST invoice with serial numbers — it is your 10-year product + 25-year performance warranty proof. Amazon/Flipkart invoices are accepted.
  • Check the price per watt: online runs ₹28–₹36/W; a local dealer in Pudukkottai/Karaikudi/Trichy may quote ₹22–₹28/W and will also install. Worth one phone call before ordering online — the saving on 400 Wp can be ₹2,000–₹4,000.
  • Prefer a panel whose datasheet you can download (PDF) before paying — that is how you confirm Voc, Isc and cell count.

4. Wiring (simple, do not change it)

5. Terrace notes for Alangudi (10.4° N, cyclone belt)

  • Tilt 10–12°, facing true south. A steep tilt is only for wind drag here, not for energy.
  • Shade check: watch where the parapet, water tank and staircase head-room cast shadows at 9 a.m. and 4 p.m. Keep panels clear of those — one shaded strip can cut a panel's output by a third.
  • Aim for ~2.5 m × 1.2 m of clear area for two 200 W panels with walking gap.
  • Galvanised structure, chemical anchors, 10–15 cm air gap under the panels. Bolt down hard — November–December winds are the design case.
  • DC SPD + good earthing (one earth pit for the frame, copper 6 mm²). Lightning is the most likely cause of a dead PCU in this district.
  • Run DC cable in conduit, keep it under ~20 m for 6 mm²; longer runs need 10 mm².

6. PCU & battery settings that protect your money

PCU

  • Charging voltage: select tubular (your Luminous 150 Ah), not flat/SMF.
  • Keep solar priority ON; the PCU runs your fans + modem + camera on solar first, then charges the battery.
  • Grid charging: ON during the June–September monsoon; OFF in the dry months (or leave it in "economy" mode) if you want the maximum EB bill saving.
  • Needs 1 ft free space on all sides — it is a fan-cooled unit.
  • Do not add a 3rd panel of a different wattage later. If you expand, buy the same model.

Battery (the part that dies early if ignored)

  • Top up with distilled water every 30–45 days in summer — 400 Wp is more charge current than mains-only charging, so it uses water faster.
  • Never let it sit discharged: your loads run 24×7, so the array must be able to refill daily — this is exactly why 2 panels is the minimum, not 1.
  • Wipe the panel glass every 2–3 weeks; Alangudi's dust months cost you 5–10 % output if ignored.
  • Expected battery life with this cycling: 3.5–5 years for a good tubular; longer if you keep water levels right.

8. Order-of-work checklist

  1. Measure the terrace: is there a 2.5 m × 1.2 m shadow-free patch at 9 a.m. and 4 p.m.? If not, adjust placement before ordering.
  2. Choose panels; download the datasheet; confirm 36 cells, Voc 22–26 V, 12 V nominal.
  3. Call one local dealer in Pudukkottai / Karaikudi / Trichy for a price on the same wattage — compare with Amazon/Flipkart/Moglix price per watt.
  4. Buy 2 identical panels + the combiner hardware in one order.
  5. Get the structure fabricated locally (any welding shop can copy the drawing above).
  6. Install in the early morning, torque the MC4s, then check the PCU's LCD: PV current should be 8–10 A per panel in good sun.
  7. First month: check water level weekly until you learn how fast this array uses it.
